## Weapons

1. The Absolute Best for Raw Power & Reliability: Maxim-Nordenfelt "Pom-Pom" (1890s)
Why it was the best: It was directly derived from Hiram Maxim's core recoil mechanism, which was the only 19th-century automatic system reliable enough to see widespread active service.

Historical Impact: It fired 37mm explosive shells at a rapid pace. During the Second Boer War (1899–1902), it caused utter shock and panic due to its explosive, continuous cadence.

In-Game Vibe: It is the ultimate heavy "tank-buster." Against high-poise medieval bosses or massive stone golems, nothing beats unloading explosive, rhythmically chugging automatic rounds.

2. The Absolute Best for Versatility & Design: Modified Mauser C96 (1896)
Why it was the best: Unlike experimental bench projects, over a million C96 handguns were produced. Its feed mechanism was fast, smooth, and robust.

Historical Impact: Attaching the wooden holster to the handle turned a standard sidearm into a high-rate-of-fire shoulder carbine.

In-Game Vibe: The best choice for aggressive, high-mobility builds. It gives players a fast-drawing machine-pistol to quickly target boss weak spots or break animations while dodging close-quarters melee swings.

The Rest (Novelties with Major Flaws)
Cei-Rigotti (1890s): Conceptually the coolest (the ancestor to modern assault rifles), but historically a mechanical nightmare. Early military trials noted severe overheating, frequent jamming, inconsistent gas pressure, and poor build quality. Great for a video game prototype, but flawed in real life.

Claxton Volley Gun: Heavy, clunky, and quickly rendered obsolete by true recoil-operated machine guns.

Game Design Recommendation
Use the Cei-Rigotti aesthetic as an experimental, rare end-game automatic rifle.

Use a shoulder-stocked C96 for agile run-and-gun combat.

Use a handheld "Pom-Pom" heavy automatic cannon as the ultimate boss-shredder.
